# HLSP As LC-AGP And The Limits Of Sign-Summed AGP-Kernel Compression

## 1. Scope

This note isolates the following mathematical question.

Given one closed-shell HLSP/APG state written as a product of bond-resolved
geminals,

$$
|\Phi\rangle
=
\hat G_1^\dagger \hat G_2^\dagger \cdots \hat G_n^\dagger |0\rangle,
$$

can one:

1. represent it exactly as a linear combination of AGP states with only a
   polynomial number of terms; and
2. if that exact LC-AGP representation is exponentially large, avoid writing it
   explicitly by projecting directly on AGP pair kernels through a sign sum,
   Walsh-Hadamard transform, or separator dynamic program?

The answer splits into two parts.

1. At the state-representation level, exact LC-AGP rank is exponential in the
   number of independent geminal factors.
2. At the matrix-element level, the sign-summed AGP-kernel route is exact, but
   by itself it does not remove the exponential dependence. Any exact reduction
   below the full sign cube must come from additional structure such as exact
   factorization or bounded separator width.

Throughout this note, the algebraic model is the standard APG one:

- the bond-resolved geminals $\hat G_\mu^\dagger$ are treated as pairwise
  commuting generators of a commutative algebra;
- one AGP state is generated by an $n$-th power of one linear combination of
  those generators.

This level of abstraction is exactly what is needed for the LC-AGP question.
It does not assume that the underlying fermionic many-electron problem has
already been compressed.

## 2. HLSP/APG And AGP In One Commutative Algebra

Let

$$
\mathcal{A} = \mathbb{C}[\hat G_1^\dagger,\ldots,\hat G_n^\dagger]
$$

be the commutative algebra generated by $n$ bond-resolved geminals.

One HLSP/APG state is the square-free degree-$n$ product

$$
\hat M_n
:=
\hat G_1^\dagger \hat G_2^\dagger \cdots \hat G_n^\dagger.
$$

One AGP generator is a linear form

$$
\hat L(\mathbf{c})
:=
\sum_{\mu=1}^n c_\mu \hat G_\mu^\dagger,
$$

and the corresponding degree-$n$ AGP object is

$$
\hat L(\mathbf{c})^n.
$$

Therefore the exact LC-AGP representation problem is:

find the smallest $R$ such that

$$
\hat M_n
=
\sum_{r=1}^R \alpha_r \hat L_r^n,
\qquad
\hat L_r = \sum_{\mu=1}^n c_{r\mu} \hat G_\mu^\dagger.
$$

If we identify

$$
\hat G_\mu^\dagger \longleftrightarrow x_\mu,
$$

then the problem becomes the ordinary Waring decomposition of the square-free
monomial

$$
x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n
=
\sum_{r=1}^R \alpha_r L_r(x)^n.
$$

So the exact LC-AGP rank of one $n$-geminal HLSP/APG state is exactly the
Waring rank of the monomial $x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n$.

## 3. Exponential LC-AGP Rank

### 3.1 Explicit Exponential Upper Bound

Define

$$
S_n(x)
:=
\sum_{\epsilon_2,\ldots,\epsilon_n \in \{\pm 1\}}
\left(\prod_{i=2}^n \epsilon_i\right)
\left(
x_1 + \epsilon_2 x_2 + \cdots + \epsilon_n x_n
\right)^n.
$$

Expand the $n$-th power:

$$
S_n(x)
=
\sum_{\substack{k_1,\ldots,k_n \ge 0 \\ k_1+\cdots+k_n=n}}
\binom{n}{k_1,\ldots,k_n}
x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_n^{k_n}
\sum_{\epsilon_2,\ldots,\epsilon_n}
\prod_{i=2}^n \epsilon_i^{k_i+1}.
$$

The inner sign sum factorizes:

$$
\sum_{\epsilon_2,\ldots,\epsilon_n}
\prod_{i=2}^n \epsilon_i^{k_i+1}

=
\prod_{i=2}^n
\left(
\sum_{\epsilon_i=\pm 1} \epsilon_i^{k_i+1}
\right).
$$

For one index $i \ge 2$,

$$
\sum_{\epsilon_i=\pm 1} \epsilon_i^{k_i+1}
=
\begin{cases}
2, & k_i+1 \text{ even}, \\
0, & k_i+1 \text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$

Hence the full sum is nonzero if and only if every $k_i$ with $i \ge 2$ is
odd. Since the total degree is $n$, this forces

$$
k_2 = k_3 = \cdots = k_n = 1,
\qquad
k_1 = 1.
$$

Therefore only the square-free monomial survives, and

$$
S_n(x)
=
2^{n-1} n! \, x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n.
$$

So we obtain the exact identity

$$
x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n
=
\frac{1}{2^{n-1} n!}
\sum_{\epsilon_2,\ldots,\epsilon_n \in \{\pm 1\}}
\left(\prod_{i=2}^n \epsilon_i\right)
\left(
x_1 + \epsilon_2 x_2 + \cdots + \epsilon_n x_n
\right)^n.
$$

After substituting back $\hat G_\mu^\dagger$ for $x_\mu$, this becomes

$$
\hat G_1^\dagger \cdots \hat G_n^\dagger
=
\frac{1}{2^{n-1} n!}
\sum_{\epsilon_2,\ldots,\epsilon_n \in \{\pm 1\}}
\left(\prod_{i=2}^n \epsilon_i\right)
\left(
\hat G_1^\dagger
+ \epsilon_2 \hat G_2^\dagger
+ \cdots
+ \epsilon_n \hat G_n^\dagger
\right)^n.
$$

This proves the upper bound

$$
R_{\mathrm{LC\text{-}AGP}}(n) \le 2^{n-1}.
$$

### 3.2 Exponential Lower Bound

Now let

$$
f(x) = \sum_{r=1}^R \alpha_r L_r(x)^n,
$$

where each $L_r(x)$ is linear. For a fixed derivative order $p$, define the
derivative span

$$
\mathcal{D}_p(f)
:=
\mathrm{span}\left\{
\partial^\beta f : |\beta| = p
\right\}.
$$

For one term $L_r(x)^n$, every $p$-th derivative is a scalar multiple of

$$
L_r(x)^{n-p},
$$

so

$$
\dim \mathcal{D}_p\!\left(L_r^n\right) \le 1.
$$

By linearity,

$$
\dim \mathcal{D}_p(f) \le R.
$$

Now take

$$
f(x) = x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n.
$$

Any $p$-th partial derivative is, up to a scalar, one of the square-free
monomials of degree $n-p$:

$$
x_{i_1} x_{i_2} \cdots x_{i_{n-p}}.
$$

There are exactly

$$
\binom{n}{p}
$$

such monomials, and they are linearly independent. Hence

$$
\dim \mathcal{D}_p(x_1 \cdots x_n) = \binom{n}{p}.
$$

Therefore any LC-AGP decomposition must satisfy

$$
R \ge \binom{n}{p}
\qquad
\text{for every } p = 0,\ldots,n.
$$

Taking $p = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ gives

$$
R_{\mathrm{LC\text{-}AGP}}(n)
\ge
\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}
\sim
\frac{2^n}{\sqrt{\pi n/2}}.
$$

So the exact LC-AGP rank is necessarily exponential.

### 3.3 Consequence

The self-contained bounds are

$$
\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}
\le
R_{\mathrm{LC\text{-}AGP}}(n)
\le
2^{n-1}.
$$

Hence exact LC-AGP representation is exponentially large in the number of
independent geminal factors.

Over $\mathbb{C}$, the standard Waring-rank theorem sharpens this to the exact
value

$$
R_{\mathrm{LC\text{-}AGP}}(n) = 2^{n-1}.
$$

For the algorithmic discussion below, the exponential lower bound already
contains the essential message.

## 4. Exact Matrix Elements As Sign-Summed AGP Kernels

The exponential LC-AGP rank does not by itself rule out direct matrix-element
compression. To analyze that question, write the Fischer decomposition on both
sides of one matrix element.

Let

$$
|\Phi_X\rangle
=
\hat G_{X,1}^\dagger \cdots \hat G_{X,n_X}^\dagger |0\rangle,
\qquad
|\Phi_Y\rangle
=
\hat G_{Y,1}^\dagger \cdots \hat G_{Y,n_Y}^\dagger |0\rangle.
$$

For sign vectors

$$
\sigma \in \{\pm 1\}^{n_X-1},
\qquad
\tau \in \{\pm 1\}^{n_Y-1},
$$

define the AGP generators

$$
\hat L_X(\sigma)
:=
\hat G_{X,1}^\dagger
+
\sum_{i=2}^{n_X} \sigma_i \hat G_{X,i}^\dagger,
$$

$$
\hat L_Y(\tau)
:=
\hat G_{Y,1}^\dagger
+
\sum_{j=2}^{n_Y} \tau_j \hat G_{Y,j}^\dagger,
$$

and the characters

$$
\chi_X(\sigma) := \prod_{i=2}^{n_X} \sigma_i,
\qquad
\chi_Y(\tau) := \prod_{j=2}^{n_Y} \tau_j.
$$

Then

$$
|\Phi_X\rangle
=
\frac{1}{2^{n_X-1} n_X!}
\sum_{\sigma \in \{\pm 1\}^{n_X-1}}
\chi_X(\sigma)\,
\hat L_X(\sigma)^{n_X}|0\rangle,
$$

$$
|\Phi_Y\rangle
=
\frac{1}{2^{n_Y-1} n_Y!}
\sum_{\tau \in \{\pm 1\}^{n_Y-1}}
\chi_Y(\tau)\,
\hat L_Y(\tau)^{n_Y}|0\rangle.
$$

Define the sign-assigned AGP states

$$
|\Psi_X(\sigma)\rangle := \hat L_X(\sigma)^{n_X}|0\rangle,
\qquad
|\Psi_Y(\tau)\rangle := \hat L_Y(\tau)^{n_Y}|0\rangle.
$$

For any operator $\hat O$, define the AGP-pair kernel

$$
K_{\hat O}(\sigma,\tau)
:=
\langle \Psi_X(\sigma) | \hat O | \Psi_Y(\tau) \rangle.
$$

Then the exact HLSP matrix element is

$$
\langle \Phi_X | \hat O | \Phi_Y \rangle
=
\frac{1}{2^{n_X-1} 2^{n_Y-1} n_X! n_Y!}
\sum_{\sigma,\tau}
\chi_X(\sigma)\chi_Y(\tau)\,
K_{\hat O}(\sigma,\tau).
$$

This is already the desired direct sign-summed AGP-kernel formula. No explicit
LC-AGP basis need be materialized.

## 5. Walsh-Hadamard Interpretation

Set

$$
m := (n_X - 1) + (n_Y - 1),
$$

and write all sign variables together as

$$
\lambda \in \{\pm 1\}^m.
$$

Then the exact matrix element has the form

$$
\mathcal{M}_{\hat O}(X,Y)
=
C_{X,Y}
\sum_{\lambda \in \{\pm 1\}^m}
\chi(\lambda)\,
F_{\hat O}(\lambda),
$$

where:

- $C_{X,Y}$ is the constant prefactor;
- $\chi(\lambda)$ is the highest-frequency Walsh character;
- $F_{\hat O}(\lambda)$ is the AGP-pair kernel evaluated at the corresponding
  sign-assigned generators.

Therefore the exact HLSP matrix element is a single Walsh-Hadamard coefficient
of the sign-cube function $F_{\hat O}$.

This reformulation is exact and important:

- it removes the need to store an exponentially large LC-AGP basis explicitly;
- but it does not yet remove the exponential summation itself.

## 6. Why Hadamard Projection Alone Does Not Remove The Exponential

The key point is that the sign-summed formula above is only a projection
identity. By itself it does not imply a compressed algorithm.

### 6.1 Multilinear Coefficient Extraction Problem

Let

$$
V_m
:=
\left\{
P(z_1,\ldots,z_m) :
\deg_{z_i} P \le 1 \text{ for all } i
\right\}
$$

be the vector space of multilinear polynomials in $m$ variables. It has
dimension

$$
\dim V_m = 2^m.
$$

For

$$
P(z) = \sum_{S \subseteq \{1,\ldots,m\}} a_S \prod_{i \in S} z_i,
$$

the top multilinear coefficient is

$$
a_{[m]}.
$$

By the standard cube-projection identity,

$$
a_{[m]}
=
\frac{1}{2^m}
\sum_{\lambda \in \{\pm 1\}^m}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^m \lambda_i\right)
P(\lambda).
$$

This is the same Walsh projection appearing in the AGP-kernel formula above.

### 6.2 Black-Box Lower Bound

Consider the full cube-evaluation map

$$
E : V_m \longrightarrow \mathbb{C}^{2^m},
\qquad
E(P) = \bigl(P(\lambda)\bigr)_{\lambda \in \{\pm 1\}^m}.
$$

Because the Walsh-Hadamard matrix is invertible, $E$ is a linear isomorphism.
Hence every linear functional on $V_m$ has a unique representation as a weighted
sum of all cube evaluations.

For the top coefficient functional $T(P) := a_{[m]}(P)$, the cube-projection
formula gives

$$
T(P)
=
\sum_{\lambda \in \{\pm 1\}^m}
w(\lambda)\,P(\lambda),
\qquad
w(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2^m}\prod_{i=1}^m \lambda_i.
$$

This weight vector is nonzero on every cube point:

$$
w(\lambda) \ne 0
\qquad
\text{for all } \lambda \in \{\pm 1\}^m.
$$

Now suppose one tries to represent the same functional using only a proper
subset $S \subsetneq \{\pm 1\}^m$:

$$
T(P)
=
\sum_{\lambda \in S} \widetilde{w}(\lambda)\,P(\lambda).
$$

Then $T$ would have two different representations in the dual basis induced by
the cube evaluations:

1. the full-support vector $w$ above;
2. the subset-supported vector $\widetilde{w}$ extended by zero outside $S$.

This contradicts uniqueness because $E$ is an isomorphism.

Therefore no proper subset of cube evaluations can recover the top multilinear
coefficient for every multilinear polynomial in $V_m$.

So in the generic black-box model:

$$
\text{exact recovery of the top Walsh coefficient requires all } 2^m
\text{ cube values.}
$$

This is a purely mathematical statement. It means that a Walsh-Hadamard
projection does not create generic exact compression on its own.

### 6.3 Interpretation For AGP Kernels

The exact HLSP matrix element is one Walsh coefficient of the AGP-kernel
function $F_{\hat O}(\lambda)$. Therefore:

- replacing explicit LC-AGP storage by a sign-summed AGP kernel is exact;
- but the sign sum is still an exponential projector unless the kernel function
  has additional structure beyond "it can be evaluated at one sign point".

So a direct sign-summed AGP-kernel route is mathematically valid, but it is not
automatically a non-exponential algorithm.

## 7. Where Exact Compression Can Still Enter

The previous section only rules out generic black-box compression. It does not
rule out compression that uses internal structure of $F_{\hat O}$.

### 7.1 Factor Graph Form

Suppose one can rewrite the sign-assigned kernel as

$$
F_{\hat O}(\lambda)
=
\prod_{a \in \mathcal{A}}
\phi_a(\lambda_{B_a}),
$$

where each factor $\phi_a$ depends only on a small subset $B_a$ of sign
variables. Then

$$
\mathcal{M}_{\hat O}(X,Y)
=
C_{X,Y}
\sum_{\lambda \in \{\pm 1\}^m}
\chi(\lambda)
\prod_{a \in \mathcal{A}}
\phi_a(\lambda_{B_a})
$$

is a discrete partition function on a factor graph.

If that factor graph has treewidth $w$, standard variable elimination or
junction-tree dynamic programming gives an exact cost of the form

$$
O\!\left(\mathrm{poly}(m)\,\gamma^w\right),
$$

where $\gamma$ depends on the local state size carried by each elimination
message.

This is the mathematical meaning of separator compression in the present
context.

### 7.2 What The Width Parameter Means

The important point is that the relevant exponent is no longer the number of
AGP terms in an LC-AGP expansion. It is the width of the interaction structure
on the sign variables.

So there are two very different quantities:

1. **State rank**

   $$
   R_{\mathrm{LC\text{-}AGP}}(n),
   $$

   which is exponential in the number of independent geminals.

2. **Kernel graph width**

   $$
   w,
   $$

   which may be much smaller if the sign-labeled kernel factorizes over a
   sparse metric-aware interaction graph.

The first quantity controls whether exact HLSP can be compressed into a small
LC-AGP basis. The second controls whether one might still evaluate exact matrix
elements without materializing that basis.

### 7.3 Why This Does Not Contradict The Rank Result

An exact separator algorithm, if it exists, would not imply a small LC-AGP
rank. It would only imply that one specific matrix-element functional can be
computed through a compressed elimination order.

That is a weaker and more realistic target than a global state-space collapse.

## 8. Bottom Line

The mathematical picture is now clean.

1. One $n$-geminal HLSP/APG state is the square-free monomial

   $$
   \hat G_1^\dagger \cdots \hat G_n^\dagger.
   $$

2. Exact LC-AGP representation is the Waring decomposition of that monomial.

3. Self-contained bounds already prove

   $$
   \binom{n}{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}
   \le
   R_{\mathrm{LC\text{-}AGP}}(n)
   \le
   2^{n-1},
   $$

   so exact LC-AGP rank is exponential.

4. The Fischer sign formula gives an exact direct matrix-element identity as a
   sign-summed AGP-pair kernel, so explicit LC-AGP storage is not necessary.

5. However, that sign sum is exactly one Walsh coefficient of a function on the
   $m$-dimensional sign cube. In the generic black-box setting, extracting that
   coefficient still requires $2^m$ information in the worst case.

6. Therefore:

   - **exact LC-AGP compression of the state is generically exponential;**
   - **direct sign-summed AGP-kernel evaluation is exact but not generically
     non-exponential;**
   - **any exact improvement below the full sign cube must come from extra
     algebraic structure, such as exact factorization or bounded separator
     width.**

In short:

$$
\text{LC-AGP rank explosion and sign-cube projection are different problems.}
$$

The first is generically exponential at the state level. The second can only be
improved by exploiting structure in the kernel over the sign variables, not by
the AGP identity alone.
